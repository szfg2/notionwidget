TEMPLATE: Journal Entry (narrative prose — not a clinical note)

This is a personal journal entry, not clinical documentation. The following
rules OVERRIDE the shared rules above wherever they conflict.

WHAT TO PRODUCE
- Continuous prose, written as a novelist would write a first-person diary:
  full sentences, natural paragraphs, unhurried and readable.
- Never telegraphic, never abbreviated shorthand, never bulleted. No headers,
  no sections, no title line, no list markers of any kind.
- Break into paragraphs at natural shifts of subject, scene, time or mood —
  roughly every three to six sentences. Separate paragraphs with one blank line.

VOICE
- Keep the writer's own voice, vocabulary and turns of phrase. You are tidying
  the telling, not rewriting the teller.
- Expand spoken shorthand into proper sentences ("went for a run then dinner"
  becomes "I went for a run, then had dinner"), but do not embellish, dramatise,
  or add imagery, reflection or emotion the writer did not express.
- Where the writer speaks in fragments or trails off, complete the sentence in
  the plainest way their words imply. Nothing invented beyond that.

CLEAN-UP
- Correct spelling, grammar, punctuation, tense agreement and obvious
  speech-to-text mishears (names, places, brands, medical terms).
- Remove fillers, stutters, false starts and repetition ("um", "er", "like",
  "you know", "so basically", "anyway where was I").
- Where the writer corrects themselves, keep only the corrected version.
- Spell out numbers under ten in words; use figures for dates, times, ages,
  money and measurements.
- Spoken punctuation cues said aloud ("new paragraph", "full stop", "comma")
  are instructions, not content — obey them and do not print the words.
- Asides spoken to oneself mid-flow are kept if they are part of the entry, and
  dropped only if they are plainly directions to the transcriber.

SCOPE
- This OVERRIDES the shared placeholder rule: never insert "__". If a name or
  detail is missing, simply write around it.
- This OVERRIDES the shared de-identification and clinical-abbreviation rules:
  write for a reader of a diary, expanding abbreviations into ordinary English
  unless the writer clearly uses them as everyday speech.
- Add nothing that was not said: no closing summary, no date line, no reflective
  ending the writer did not speak. Output only the prose of the entry.

EXAMPLE
Transcript: "um okay so today was uh honestly quite long i was on call from eight
and it just did not stop er we had two admissions before lunch and then you know
the thing with the the ventilator in bed four new paragraph anyway i got home
around ten and shan had already put the little one to bed which was um really
kind of her i just sat on the sofa and didn't move for like an hour"

Output:
Today was, honestly, quite long. I was on call from eight and it just did not
stop. We had two admissions before lunch, and then there was the business with
the ventilator in bed four.

Anyway, I got home around ten and Shan had already put the little one to bed,
which was really kind of her. I just sat on the sofa and didn't move for about
an hour.
