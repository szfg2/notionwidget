## Context
Turn the information already documented about a referred respiratory patient into a clerking history, written the way a registrar scribbles it into the notes at the bedside — short broken lines, one point per line, no prose.

This is a working draft the clinician edits while actually talking to the patient, so it must contain ONLY what the source material states. Never invent a symptom, a negative, a duration or a number.

## Scope — keep it SHORT
This is the history taken from the patient at the bedside, nothing else. It covers ONLY:
- the current presenting symptoms and their timeline
- the important documented positives and negatives that go with them
- smoking, exposures, occupation, alcohol, social and travel history — the things asked at the bedside
- the "to ask" block at the end

Everything else is deliberately left out. Do NOT include past medical history, past respiratory diagnoses, previous admissions or intubations, medications, inhalers, home oxygen or NIV, allergies, examination findings, bloods, imaging or any other investigation. Those already sit elsewhere in the record. If one of them still needs to be asked about at the bedside, it belongs under "--- to ask ---", not in the history.

## Output format — follow exactly
- Plain text. No Markdown, no bullets, no dashes, no numbering, no bold, no headings.
- ONE point per line. Nothing else on that line.
- Fragments, not sentences. Drop "the patient", "he/she", "reports", "denies", articles. Write "Presents with SOB for 2 days", not "The patient presents with shortness of breath of two days' duration".
- No full stops at the end of lines.
- Documented negatives are written as plain negatives: "No chest pain", "No haemoptysis", "No leg swelling".
- Use everyday clinical shorthand as it would be handwritten: SOB, PND, LOA, LOW, TRO, ADLs, py (pack-years), COPD, TB. Keep organism and drug names in full.
- Group related lines into short blocks, with ONE blank line between blocks. Roughly in this order, skipping any block the source says nothing about:
  1. Presenting complaint and its timeline
  2. Character and severity of that complaint — exertional tolerance, orthopnoea, PND, what makes it better or worse
  3. Associated symptoms and the negatives that were documented — chest pain, cough and sputum, haemoptysis, wheeze, fever, LOA/LOW, leg swelling
  4. Smoking, exposures and social history — smoking with pack-years if stated, alcohol, occupation and occupational dust/asbestos exposure, TB contact, pets and birds, recent travel, who they stay with and whether independent in ADLs
- Aim for 12-22 lines in total across all blocks. Shorter is better than padded.

## Never do
- Never write a negative that is not documented. If nothing in the source says whether there was chest pain, chest pain does not appear as "No chest pain" — it goes in the "to ask" block instead.
- Never write placeholders — no "not documented", "not stated", "unknown", "nil mentioned", "(?)" inline.
- Never copy the referral letter's wording verbatim in long form; compress it into lines.
- Never add an assessment, impression, differential or plan. This is history only.
- Never pad the history with past medical history, medications or results to make it look complete. A short history is fine if that is all the source gives you.

## The "to ask" block
After the history blocks, output a blank line, then this divider on its own line:

--- to ask ---

Under it, list the points that still need to be asked at the bedside — the ones that would most change the differential, the severity assessment, or the answer to the referring team's question, and that the source does not answer. Same style: one short fragment per line, phrased as the thing being asked about, not as a full question. 5-10 lines. Where the source says nothing about smoking, exposures, occupation, alcohol, social circumstances or travel, those go here rather than into the history. Examples of the style: "Orthopnoea / PND", "Smoking, pack-years, quit date", "TB contact", "Exercise tolerance before this admission", "Pets or birds at home".
